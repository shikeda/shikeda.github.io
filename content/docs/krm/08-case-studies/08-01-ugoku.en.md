# Investigating Wakun "Ugoku"

Ikeda Shoju

December 22–26, 2024

## Summary

This page is an English-language summary of [和訓「ウゴク」の調査](/docs/krm/08-case-studies/08-01-ugoku/), a Japanese case study investigating attestations of the *wakun* "*ugoku*" (ウゴク) in the Kanchi-in manuscript of the *Ruiju Myōgishō* (KRM). The full account, including the character-by-character evidence, is available only in Japanese; this page presents only the abstract.

This note compiles attestations of the *wakun* "*ugoku*" (ウゴク) and its related forms "*ugokasu*" (ウゴカス), "*ogoku*" (オゴク), and "*ogokasu*" (オゴカス) in KRM, as reference material for annotation work. KRM has 34 characters bearing the single-character *wakun* "*ugoku*," of which 11 carry a confirmed tone mark. Cross-checking these against the *hyōki* (表記, "orthographic forms") column of the *Nihon Kokugo Daijiten*, 2nd edition (*Nikkoku*) found 27 already listed there and 7 not listed. Of the 7 unlisted characters, six — 頷, 振, 掉, 忧, 躁, and 戁 — have textual grounds (a gloss of "動也," "means to move") in Chinese-side dictionaries such as the *Shuowen Jiezi*, the *Tenrei Banshō Meigi*, the *Yupian*, and the *Guangyun*; 振 in particular also carries the same reading in the *Iroha Jiruishō*, suggesting *Nikkoku*'s omission is a simple oversight. 債 and 搯 can likewise be explained as confusions with the graphically similar characters 僨 and 掐, so all seven could reasonably be added to *Nikkoku*'s column. 化, however, has no "動也" gloss traceable in Chinese sources, and its "*ugoku*" reading is suspected to be a conflation with the graphically similar character 吪.

The note then compares KRM against the three-volume *Iroha Jiruishō* and surveys actual attestations in the *Kunten Goi Shūsei* and the *Gunsho Chiyō Keibu Goi Sakuin*, finding "*ugoku*" directly confirmed for only 9 and 4 characters respectively — far fewer than KRM's 34. For the related forms, KRM records 29 characters with "*ugokasu*," 32 with "*ogoku*," and 3 with "*ogokasu*," though corroboration from the *Iroha Jiruishō* or *kunten* materials is limited. Finally, cross-referencing the characters glossed "動也" in the *Erya*'s *Shigu* and *Shixun* chapters and the *Guangya*'s *Shigu* I found that 6 of the *Erya*'s 9 such characters and 10 of the *Guangya*'s 28 match KRM's "*ugoku*"-type *wakun*.

The investigation concludes that, for annotating KRM's *wakun* — not only "*ugoku*" — checking the Chinese dictionaries and rime books (*Shuowen Jiezi*, *Erya*, *Guangya*, *Yupian*, *Guangyun*) for textual grounds, and, where needed, tracing the philological lineage through Morohashi's *Dai Kan-Wa Jiten* and the *Guxun Huizuan*, is an effective method.
