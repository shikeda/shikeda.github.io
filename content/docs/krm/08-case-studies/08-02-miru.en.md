# Investigating Wakun "Miru"

Ikeda Shoju

January 4–11, 2025

## Summary

This page is an English-language summary of [和訓「ミル」の調査](/docs/krm/08-case-studies/08-02-miru/), a Japanese case study investigating attestations of the *wakun* "*miru*" (ミル) in the Kanchi-in manuscript of the *Ruiju Myōgishō* (KRM). The full account, including the character-by-character evidence, is available only in Japanese; this page presents only the abstract.

This note uses the *wakun* "*miru*" (ミル) as a test case to examine the reliability of the *hyōki* (表記, "orthographic forms") column in the *Nihon Kokugo Daijiten*, 2nd edition (*Nikkoku*). After tabulating the source editions and dates of the 17 pre-modern dictionaries cited in *Nikkoku*'s own dictionary-source column, I confirmed that 15 of them, including KRM, do record "*miru*," but found that the *Shinsen Jikyō* — despite being listed — does not actually attest the reading, an error in *Nikkoku*'s source citations. *Nikkoku*'s *hyōki* column lists 104 attestations for "*miru*" (103 single-character, 1 compound), of which 53 characters (about half) were entered as bitmap images in the 2007 JapanKnowledge edition because they could not be input in Unicode at the time; nearly all can now be represented in Unicode. Checking these against the Kanchi-in manuscript's text turned up seven apparent input errors (e.g., 呪 should be 眖) and one omission — 瞩, which does appear in KRM but is missing from the *hyōki* column. Cases of apparent confusion between graphically similar characters, such as 覞 and 䁤, were also examined individually.

Surveying, in order of appearance, the characters in KRM bearing "*miru*" that are not listed in *Nikkoku*'s *hyōki* column turned up over 100 characters: 51 have textual grounds confirmable in dictionaries such as the *Shuowen Jiezi*, the *Tenrei Banshō Meigi*, and the *Guangyun*, and could reasonably be added to the column; 11 appear to be misreadings caused by graphic confusion with similar characters; and no textual basis could be identified for the remaining 32. A comparison with the *Ekirin-bon Setsuyōshū* also found that 9 of *Nikkoku*'s compound-word attestations do not appear in that dictionary's "Bi" (美) and "Genji" (言辞) sections.

In conclusion: (1) the *hyōki* column's entries generally have reliable semantic correspondence; (2) it nonetheless contains outright errors (6 characters); (3) it has room for defensible additions (about 50 characters); (4) some entries are misreadings from graphically similar characters (11); and (5) some entries have no identifiable textual basis (32). Identifying character forms and judging the correctness of glyph input are unavoidable tasks in annotating KRM; the supporting evidence for each case is recorded in the `remarks` column of `KRM_wakun.tsv`.
