# Homograph Distinction

Ikeda Shoju

January 13 – February 11, 2025

## Summary

This page is an English-language summary of [同仮名異語の区別](/docs/krm/08-case-studies/08-04-kana-split/), a Japanese case study investigating the distinction between homographs (distinct words sharing the same kana spelling) in the Kanchi-in manuscript of the *Ruiju Myōgishō* (KRM). The full account, including the character-by-character evidence, is available only in Japanese; this page presents only the abstract.

This note uses the *wakun* "*tsuku*" (ツク) as a test case for distinguishing homographs. Whereas the earlier investigations of "*ugoku*" and "*miru*" focused mainly on verifying the *hyōki* column of the *Nihon Kokugo Daijiten*, 2nd edition (*Nikkoku*), this note takes a different approach, using the three-volume *Iroha Jiruishō* (the Maeda and Kurokawa manuscripts) as a lens for examining KRM's *wakun*. A survey of *Nikkoku* and the *Kadokawa Kogo Daijiten* found 14 and 10 separate headword entries for "*tsuku*," and 4 and 2 for "*tsugu*," respectively — evidence of substantial homography. Sorting KRM's "*tsuku*" and "*tsugu*" attestations by the *Nikkoku* IDs assigned in `KRM_wakun.tsv` split them into several distinct word groups (e.g., "付・着・就・即・憑" and "突・衝・撞・搗・舂・築・吐"), while 60 "*tsuku*" instances and 1 "*tsugu*" instance could not yet be assigned an ID and still need sorting.

The note then tabulates the *Iroha Jiruishō*'s *tsu* (ツ) entries by meaning group — *fu* (付, 54 characters), *tsugi* (次, 51), *totsu* (突, 30), *tsuke* (漬, 1), *tsugeru* (告, 19), and *tsuku*/"pound" (舂, 8), plus *zuku* (ヅク, "owl," 1) — cross-referencing each character's location in KRM and its attestation in *Nikkoku*'s *hyōki* column against the annotations in Satō Kiyoji's *Iroha Jiruishō Ryakuchū* and the *Jigi Jikun Jiten*. For each group, a consistent set of counts was tallied: how many characters the two reference works annotate, how many appear in the *Iroha Jiruishō* but not in KRM, and how many are missing from *Nikkoku*'s *hyōki* column; for the *fu* (付) group, for example, the two works together annotate 52 of 54 characters (96%). Characters left unannotated by both works — such as 㧢, 扔, and 戀 — were individually traced through sources including the *Guxun Huizuan*, the *Guangyun*, and the *Tenrei Banshō Meigi*. For *zuku* (ヅク, "owl"), the *wakun* readings of six KRM headwords, including 巧婦 and 𪃪鵒, were investigated through the *Erya* and the *Shinsen Jikyō*, and the Unicode variant forms of 兔 were also sorted out.

The investigation ranged widely and could not be brought to a tidy conclusion, so this note is left as a stopping point rather than a finished study.
